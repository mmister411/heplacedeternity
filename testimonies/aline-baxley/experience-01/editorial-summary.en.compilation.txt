Aline Baxley’s near-death experience (NDE) is detailed in various sources, highlighting her remarkable story. Aline, who had been a backslidden Christian, reportedly died at the age of 40 and went to Hell. This experience is described in her testimony titled “I Walked in Hell.” Following a car accident in 1974, which marked her life-changing NDE, Aline Baxley dedicated her life to missionary evangelism. Her efforts have been focused on sharing her testimony globally. The authenticity of her experience is further supported by the doctor who attended her in the Emergency Room. He attested to her unexpected survival and recovery as a modern-day miracle and confirmed that she was clinically dead for nearly five minutes    .

Aline Baxley's near-death experience is marked by profound and vivid experiences that she narrates in detail. Prior to her accident, she lived a life of sin, struggling with alcohol and drug addiction, and at one point, she expressed to her mother that she was not afraid of Hell. Her mother prayed for her to experience a portion of her own Hell to turn her back to God. This led to her car accident, resulting in severe injuries, including a broken neck, major spinal breaks, and crushed organs.
In her NDE, Baxley found herself in a dark, terrifying valley, which she described as the Valley of the Shadow of Death. She experienced intense fear and a sense of being utterly lost, unable to see even her hands before her. During this time, she felt that Jesus Christ interceded on her behalf to God.
She then describes witnessing a vision of Hell, characterized by darkness, screaming souls, and gnashing of teeth. In this vision, she realized the truth of the second death, where the soul faces eternal judgment. She saw a great gulf fixed between God and the souls in Hell, the only passage through which was the blood of Jesus Christ.
While in a coma, she experienced a vision of God speaking to her, emphasizing that the gifts and calling of God are irrevocable. She also saw a scroll in Heaven, revealing her life and sins, particularly highlighting the sin of lust, which she initially refused to accept.
Her experience also included a metaphorical rebirth. She felt herself in the spiritual womb of her mother, where she witnessed the intense prayers of her mother and sister for her salvation. This vision culminated in a symbolic rebirth and a renewed call to preach God's word.
Baxley's experience emphasizes the reality of both Heaven and Hell in her narrative. She describes Hell as a place of eternal torment, where the soul experiences unending suffering, a stark contrast to the joy and light of Heaven. Her testimony underscores the importance of choosing one's eternal destination while still alive
