## Evidentiary grammar

A torment description can carry several distinct claims:

1. **Agent:** who or what acts (demon, fellow occupant, fire, current, unknown force, or the person experiencing it).
2. **Instrument:** spear, claw, chain, pitchfork, heat, bodily action, deception, or another implement.
3. **Operation:** impalement, pulling, throwing, submersion, consumption, mockery, reenactment, or restraint.
4. **Consequence:** pain, destruction or apparent restoration of flesh, lost faculties, remembered guilt, or disorientation.
5. **Recurrence:** one continuing action, multiple episodes, a closed loop, an environment reset, or regeneration between episodes.
6. **Attributed cause:** a guide's explanation, an occupant's account, the narrator's inference, or a visible inscription.

A scene can occupy several categories, but an instrument merely present is not an instrument necessarily used to pierce; a single encounter is not automatically an endless cycle; and observing another person's suffering is not being personally subjected to it. The occurrence is attributed to its witness throughout.

## III. Earthly roles reflected or reenacted

The family formerly labeled *Earthly roles repeatedly enacted* is now called **Earthly roles reflected or reenacted**. Its membership remains nine records across five testimony units. The new name captures two distinct phenomena:

- **Activity continued by an occupant:** a former preacher reads or preaches (BAX-044, BAX-046, AZ3-012), singers are compelled to sing (AZ1-021, AZ2-017), a pastor appears to continue drunken behavior (AZ3-018), and former rivals fight (AZ3-021).
- **Setting or professional function represented in a punishment scene:** Melvin's simulated revival with a minister (MEL-021) and his professor writing at a blackboard (MEL-033).

These passages do not all assert a timed or endlessly resetting performance. The term *reflected* keeps an encountered set-piece distinguishable from an occupant actually repeating an action.

### Source-description corrections

Two curated descriptions were stronger than their passages:

- **MEL-033, line 2030:** A professor is seen writing at a blackboard. The supplied excerpt does not say that the writing is repeatedly performed. Its lemma and description now state the observed action without manufacturing recurrence.
- **AZ3-018, line 3236:** A pastor gestures as though drinking. Later narration says that the drunken behavior persists from life, but repeated drinking **gestures** are not directly described. The record now preserves this difference.

The corrected wording is synchronized in the curated phenomena seed, JSONL register, narrative register, and second-order index. Record identifiers and taxonomic assignments are unchanged.

## IV. Witness suffering versus observed suffering

The *Witness temporarily subjected to torment* family now contains ten records across four units: Baxter, Zambrano II, Wiese, and Purser. Eligible firsthand experiences include constraint, bodily injury, rats biting, extreme deprivation, apparent abandonment, pulling force, and current-driven submersion.

**Corrections:**

- **PUR-022, line 4318:** The narrator observes decomposed, motionless bodies near a pool, not injury to himself in that record.
- **PUR-025, line 4334:** The narrator observes damaged bodies of other people following immersion, not a wound personally sustained in that record.

Both remain in the register. They no longer count as records that the witness himself underwent the torment described.

## V. Correspondence between conduct and punishment

The *Punishment corresponding to acts* motif contains nine records across three testimony units after audit. The evidence divides into:

- **Narrator's interpretation:** Melvin describes individualized recompense and interprets a historical figure's torments as reciprocal to earthly atrocities (MEL-019, MEL-024).
- **Guide or encountered person's causal explanation:** the Daniel Ekechukwu and Zambrano records attribute a condition or verdict to failures of forgiveness, abuse of office, pride, rivalry, or other conduct (DAN-008; AZ3-002, AZ3-005, AZ3-013, AZ3-021).
- **Staged representation:** scenes of former professional or habitual conduct may illustrate an attributed cause without demonstrating a uniquely prescribed instrument (MEL-033, AZ3-018).

**Correction:** PAR-008, line 3653, describes the narrator learning that his deeds were divided into 132 categories. That is a **record of conduct**, not a scene of a punishment enacted in proportion to it. The record remains in the judicial/ledger material but is removed from this punishment-correspondence family.

Causal attributions must remain attached to their speaker. A narrated claim of moral fit does not by itself establish a universal penalty schedule.

## VI. Personalized or assigned confinement

After removing two contextual entries, the *Personalized or assigned confinement* family contains four records from two testimony units:

- **Melvin:** a minister's cell imitates a revival, and the witness recognizes a separate enlarged open cell as intended for himself (MEL-020, MEL-026, MEL-034; lines 2031, 2038).
- **Zambrano IV:** cells bear individual names and numbers (AZ4-005; line 3468).

**Corrections:** BRE-018 concerns differential severity according to prior religious knowledge, not confinement assignment. BAX-023 is attributed speech measuring a cell block's height, not a description of a specific prisoner's assigned room. Both remain valid register entries outside this motif.

## Scriptural correspondence boundary

**Revelation 14:11** and other passages cited by witnesses address ongoing judgment or absence of rest. They do not specify the precise regrowth interval, the professor's blackboard, the shape of an individual cell, or the implements described in these testimonies. Likewise, passages on greater accountability should be treated as principles until exegetically assessed; the atlas must not convert a witness's claimed punishment mechanism into a direct scriptural description.
