# Atlas of Perdition
## Corpus survey, provisional taxonomy, and source register

**Working edition:** 16 November 2026  
**Primary compilation:** `Pasted text(20261116-025409).txt` (4,474 lines; approximately 117,700 words).  
**Status:** First complete structural and mechanical lexical sift, accompanied by targeted close reading across the principal witnesses. This is a *corpus survey*, not a claim that all 117,700 words have received line-by-line interpretive reading, a verification of supernatural events, an exhaustive critical edition, or a finished biblical exegesis.

### Governing object

*Atlas of Perdition* documents the **reported conditions, forms, places, inhabitants, processes, and judgments of Perdition** across testimonies. A source's description remains a **reported observation** until it is evaluated. A quotation attributed to Jesus or an angel remains **speech attributed by the witness**, not an independent scriptural proposition. A reference to Scripture is an **interpretive claim by the source** until the passage and its context have been checked.

The atlas should preserve five layers:

1. **Witness:** Who speaks, through what text or editor, and under what circumstances?
2. **Phenomenon:** Exactly what was seen, heard, smelled, felt, known, or said?
3. **Relation:** Where was it, what accompanied it, what did it do, and to whom?
4. **Comparison:** What is independently or apparently repeated, what differs, and what may share textual ancestry?
5. **Scriptural correspondence:** What the cited passage explicitly says, what can be drawn from it, and what remains unique to a testimony.

Heavenly scenes, earthly visions, biographies, and eschatological predictions enter the register only when they mark the boundary of an infernal event, explain a witness's mode of access, or distinguish Hell, Hades, the abyss, and the lake of fire.

## II. Working taxonomy

Use **independent axes**, because a single object can belong to more than one: a snake may be an inhabitant, an instrument, and a recurring punishment; a river may be a topographic feature, a transport process, and a burning medium.

### A. Access and transition (`ENT`)

| Code | Feature | Description | Sources with clear examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ENT.01 | Sudden displacement | Appearing at an infernal location without a depicted route | WIE, DAN |
| ENT.02 | Tunnel descent | Downward dark passage, sometimes cold, hot, rapid, or constricting | BRE, BAX, FER, AZ1–AZ2, PER |
| ENT.03 | Funnel / vortex | Rotating funnel-like aperture or delivery path | BAX, MEL |
| ENT.04 | Opened ground / shaft | Hole appearing under or before a witness | AZ1, PUR |
| ENT.05 | Gate / door / threshold | Fixed boundary traversed with a guide, key, or command | DAN, PER, FER, BAX |
| ENT.06 | Route / divergent road | Walking or processing toward judgment, cells, or final destination | MEL, BRE, APY, AZ1, FER |
| ENT.07 | Guided versus captive entrance | Carried by angel/Christ, escorted by hostile being, or cast down alone | DAN/BAX; BRE/MEL; PUR |

### B. Macro-topography (`TOP`)

| Code | Feature | Distinctions worth retaining |
|---|---|---|
| TOP.01 | Pit / shaft / abyss | Individual bowl, central bottomless shaft, broad burning pit; these are not equivalent forms. |
| TOP.02 | Cell / chamber / dungeon | Stone, earth, barred metal, simulated room, numbered cell; isolation or communal occupation. |
| TOP.03 | Cell-block arrangement | Circular multi-tier block (BAX); stacked cubes around spiral shaft (MEL); dispersed cells (BRE, AZ). |
| TOP.04 | Valley / plain / plateau | Exterior-seeming desolation, interior basin, or staging ground. |
| TOP.05 | Road / street | Processional movement or moral branching; also a staging area for demons. |
| TOP.06 | River / lake / pool | Fire, blood-and-fire, liquid-like flame, swamp, cauldron, mud, oil; keep medium distinct. |
| TOP.07 | Mountain / volcano | Steep climb, lava outflow, slopes and height differences (PUR). |
| TOP.08 | Cavern / tunnel / vaulted space | Ceiling and enclosing rock; not synonymous with the entrance route. |
| TOP.09 | Wall / edge / gulf | Boundary of confinement, edge of a pit, chasm between states. |
| TOP.10 | Anthropomorphic Hell | Body plan: left and right legs, arms, belly, heart, jaws; specific to BAX's large-scale model. |
| TOP.11 | Functional center / depth | Claims of intensified suffering at particular depths or a center (BAX, MEL, PUR). |
| TOP.12 | Transitional holding / final lake | Witness assertions about Hades or Hell preceding final judgment versus the lake of fire; record separately. |

| Code | Feature | Attested forms |
|---|---|---|
| SEN.01 | Light and darkness | Near-total blackness; dim yellow or red light; brief local illumination; fire light swallowed by gloom. |
| SEN.02 | Thermal conditions | Terrible heat, scorching air, fire, hot liquid, occasional cold or damp pockets. |
| SEN.03 | Odor | Sulfur/burning tires, rotten flesh, sewage, stale waste, putrefaction, toxic air. |
| SEN.04 | Dryness / thirst | Lack of water, parched mouth, evaporating tears; some sources also describe deceptive water. |
| SEN.05 | Human sound | Screams, groans, pleas, curses, continual lamentation; PUR reports silence of human voices in its principal region. |
| SEN.06 | Nonhuman sound | Mocking laughter, roars, clicks, wing noise, doors, chains, rushing fire. |
| SEN.07 | Enhanced perception | Acute hearing/smell and knowledge of distances or lives without ordinary observation. |
| SEN.08 | Altered temporality | Endless repetition, reported time without ordinary sequence, disparity between elapsed earthly and visionary time. |
| SEN.09 | Material texture | Dust that feels rotten (MEL); soft, insect-filled soil (PUR); clay, stone, metal, mud, oil. |

### D. Inhabitants and agencies (`ENTY`)

1. **Human condemned:** identifiable by personal history, former vocation, name, or reported moral designation; sometimes anonymous multitudes.
2. **Human-form states:** flesh-bearing, burned skeleton, gray or mist-like inner soul, disintegrating/regenerating body, self-aware figure without ordinary organs.
3. **Escort or guide:** angel, Jesus, unknown being, reptilian deceiver, hostile captor; distinguish the witness's identification from visible form.
4. **Guard / tormentor:** prison keeper, pit patrol, implement bearer, mutilator, restraining demon, mocker.
5. **Serpentine:** ordinary giant snake, spined snake, venomous colored serpent, reptilian humanoid, lion-serpent hybrid.
6. **Arthropod / vermiform:** worm, beetle, fly, spider, scorpion, roach; size and behavior vary.
7. **Zoomorphic / composite:** winged, horned, hoofed, bat-like, scaled, bear-sized, distorted or asymmetrical figures.
8. **Deceptive appearing forms:** imitated familiar people, attractive beings, humanoid figures and transformed scenery.
9. **Named authorities:** Satan, the creature called Yama/king of hell in APY, a putative chief in MEL; identity and command chain cannot be merged by appearance alone.

### E. Custody, implements, and inscriptions (`CUS`)

`CUS.01` bar and lock; `CUS.02` chain/shackle/rope; `CUS.03` individual cell assignment; `CUS.04` pit-guard return; `CUS.05` spear/pitchfork/talon/blade/claw; `CUS.06` hook and suspension; `CUS.07` numbering and plaques; `CUS.08` mark or written accusation; `CUS.09` alleged book or roster of names; `CUS.10` barrier impossible to cross.

The plaques and forehead marking (including **666**) occur prominently in the Zambrano testimony and in the older notebook excerpts attributed to the Colombian youths. They are **not** a universal observation in this attached corpus.

### F. Somatic condition and operations (`SOM`, `OPR`)

- **Composition:** bloodless flesh; gray or black bone; mist-like soul within a skeleton; residual clothes; bodily visibility without ordinary eyes.
- **Damage:** burning, dismemberment, stripping of flesh, piercing, crushing, corrosion, bites, disease or infestation.
- **Restoration:** flesh or entire body returns before the next cycle (DAN, BAX, MEL, PUR); timings differ or are absent.
- **Fixed versus fluid identity:** recognizable mortal features retained; forms shift between ages or sexes; false visual appearances.
- **Personalized reversal:** the punishment re-enacts an offense, amplifies its consequence, or subjects the perpetrator to similar harm (MEL, BAX, AZ3).
- **Psychological continuity:** memory, conscience, recognition, regret, concern for living kin, fear, frustrated escape.
- **Environmental violence:** heat and waterlessness even without direct hostile contact; spatial surfaces themselves may injure.
- **Social torment:** mockery, betrayal, blame and the lack of compassion; some scenes isolate every individual.

### G. Judicial and moral classification (`JUD`)

The corpus repeatedly associates suffering with **unrepented sin, religious hypocrisy, abuse of entrusted office, harm done to others, rejection of warning, idolatry, deceit, and refusal to forgive**. These are **witness-attributed charges**. The atlas must not turn the list of incidents into an authoritative, exhaustive sentencing code. Distinguish an offense depicted in a narrative from a rule explicitly established in Scripture. Record purported degrees of torment as claims and assess them against texts on proportional accountability.

### H. Witness access and epistemology (`EPI`)

- **Seen:** the narrator reports the sight, form, or action.
- **Heard:** a voice, scream, or reported declaration.
- **Felt / smelled / tasted:** embodied or quasi-embodied sensation.
- **Known without ordinary mediation:** instantaneous identification, inferred history, distance, sentence, or meaning.
- **Shown by another:** a screen, scene of mortal life, scroll or divine explanation.
- **Interpreted afterward:** reflection attributed to the witness rather than present vision.
- **Editorial addition:** caption, bracketed explanation, biblical annotation, publisher's introduction.
- **Scriptural analogy:** biblical verse used to name or make sense of an experience, independent of the original text's literary setting.

This last axis is indispensable. MEL explicitly reports simultaneous access to a prisoner's experience and to an outside perspective; PAR describes sector-like spotlit visions; AZ1 alternates the witness's voice with Maxima's; WIE mixes remembered sensations with subsequent concordance work. They do not share one mode of knowing.

**Do not count as corroboration without a dependency inquiry:**

- The **woman/witch in a rocking chair** appears in BAX chapter 8 and BRE's testimony. Both contain a cell and dramatic transformation. Its distinctiveness warrants textual comparison before counting two independent sightings.
- **MEL's infernal cells and guided tour**, **BAX's cell blocks**, and later summaries may share Christian testimonial vocabulary or publications. Shared imagery alone cannot establish independent origin.
- The Daniel Ekechukwu excerpts (DAN and DA2) are two presentations of one witness.
- Four Angelica accounts belong to one witness, despite being separate episodes.
- Summary entries should never contribute additional attestation counts alongside the long version they summarize.

### Divergent reports that must remain visible

1. **Visible flames:** DAN describes torments as if fire were present without seeing flames; BAX, WIE, BRE, PUR and others describe abundant visible fire. This is a difference in reported environment, not a detail to silently reconcile.
2. **Human cries:** Almost all accounts report human cries; PUR asserts that the condemned in its region do not speak or even groan. The exact scope of PUR's observation matters.
3. **Spatial models:** BAX describes a body-shaped Hell; MEL describes stacked cells around a central shaft; PUR describes a grid of pits and volcanic rivers. The supplied text does not demonstrate their geometrical connection.
4. **Appearance of condemned:** DAN reports bodies resembling earthly human bodies; BAX often reports skeletons with gray soul-mist; PUR reports repeatedly destroyed and reformed forms. Do not impose one invariant anatomy.
5. **Location and eschatological stage:** Some sources locate Hell at the earth's center, some call a region Hades, some call a fiery feature the lake of fire, and several assert a future transfer. The terms and time stages need passage-by-passage control.
6. **Temporal sequence:** PER says past/present/future do not operate there while also mentioning living acquaintances; PAR reports a subjective journey of days within about 70 earthly minutes. These are separately located claims about experience, not measurements of one universal clock.
7. **Authority titles:** APY uses a named cultural figure, Yama, who claims to be a king of hell; WIE applies the phrase *king of terrors* to the devil, while the user's earlier composite assigns that title to the Living God. Job 18:14 itself does not name either referent. Distinct uses must stay distinct.

## V. Sample atlas entries

These model the final reference form. They are not claims that every witness saw an identical object.

### ATL-TOP-002 — Prison cell

**Type:** Place / enclosure / custody.  
**Source anchors:** BAX, chapter 8 (approximately lines 903–953); MEL (2015–2041); WIE (approximately 3889–3950); AZ1–AZ3; BRE.  
**Description:** A bounded space in which condemned persons experience repeated torment. BAX describes earth-cut cells around high circular walkways; MEL describes a chamber that appears to be a familiar house and later cells forming stacked tiers; WIE begins inside a stone cell with bars.  
**Properties:** fixed enclosure; controlled access; varying dimensions and materials; some cells tailored to an individual.  
**Scriptural relation:** Proverbs 7:27 (phrase “chambers of death”); Isaiah 24:22 (imprisonment imagery). Neither text supplies the architectural detail.  
**Status:** recurring class, variable morphology.

### ATL-ENT-003 — Funnel or vortex gateway

**Type:** Access / motion / topology.  
**Source anchors:** BAX chapter 1 (around lines 392–460); MEL (around 2013–2025).  
**Description:** A spinning or funnel-like pathway through which the witness or condemned persons arrive. BAX speaks of rotating funnels protruding around the earth; MEL reports a tunnel-like vortex and later other vortexes dropping arrivals into cells.  
**Scriptural relation:** no supplied passage explicitly specifies a rotating, physical entrance to Hades. The abyss's shaft in Revelation 9 is a separate image.  
**Status:** shared visual family; independent origin and exact mechanism unestablished.

### ATL-TOP-006 — Burning basin, lake, and river

**Type:** Place / medium / torment.  
**Source anchors:** BAX chapters 2, 7 and 13; BRE; APY; FER; WIE; PUR (4313–4430).  
**Description:** Reports of persons in fire-filled pits, fluid heat, lakes, or flowing rivers. PUR distinguishes small basins from larger volcanic rivers and claims different regeneration cycles. BAX depicts both pits and a river of blood and fire; BRE mentions boiling oil.  
**Scriptural relation:** Revelation 20–21 explicitly names a lake of fire; the varying liquid types and measured basin structures are witness-specific.  
**Status:** broad recurrence with numerous distinct subclasses.

### ATL-SEN-005 — Human lamentation

**Type:** Audible environment / speech.  
**Source anchors:** DAN, BAX, BRE, FER, WIE, KNI; contrast PUR (4320–4390).  
**Description:** Most witnesses report persistent cries, pleas or blasphemies. PUR specifically reports a region with laughter from tormentors and no audible human speech.  
**Scriptural relation:** Matthew 8:12 and related passages use weeping and gnashing of teeth; Luke 16 portrays speech from the rich man.  
**Status:** widespread but not uniform; report locality.

### ATL-ENTY-005 — Serpentine entity

**Type:** Creature / implement / agent.  
**Source anchors:** BAX, MEL, BRE, PAR, APY, AZ1–AZ3, WIE, PUR.  
**Description:** Large snakes, spined or venomous varieties, reptiles with humanoid traits, and a lion-serpent composite are all described. Some are environmental hazards; others directly restrain or attack.  
**Scriptural relation:** the serpent is a strong biblical image, but not every visionary snake is identified in Scripture as an infernal species.  
**Status:** convergent shape; diverse agency.

### ATL-SOM-004 — Bodily destruction and restoration

**Type:** Process / bodily state.  
**Source anchors:** DAN, BAX, MEL, PUR, portions of AZ3.  
**Description:** A body suffers catastrophic injury, then returns to a condition capable of further sensation. PUR gives a ten-minute cycle in one region and a much shorter one in a river; such timing is local to PUR's claim.  
**Scriptural relation:** passages on unending punishment or undying worm do not describe the sequence or timing of bodily regeneration.  
**Status:** multiple descriptive instances; physiology remains testimony-specific.

### ATL-JUD-003 — Wrongdoing repeated as punishment

**Type:** Judicial process / narrative structure.  
**Source anchors:** MEL (minister, Nazis, Hitler); BAX (witch's changing form); AZ3 (various personalized cases).  
**Description:** Reported suffering mirrors a past act, guilty desire, deceptive power, or moral injury. MEL's account is unusually explicit in presenting reciprocal punishment and foreknowledge of the condemned's lives.  
**Scriptural relation:** biblical judgment according to deeds is a broad principle (Revelation 20:12–13); the precise tableaux are not supplied by the text.  
**Status:** recurrent moral architecture, varied realization.

### ATL-EPI-002 — Knowledge of an unknown life history

**Type:** Witness access / epistemology.  
**Source anchors:** MEL (around 2023–2037); PAR; AZ1–AZ3; BAX.  
**Description:** The narrator receives information about a person's earthly life without normal investigation; sometimes this is a complete inward knowing, sometimes a displayed vision or a guide's explanation.  
**Status:** an asserted mode of perception, not independent verification of the biography.

## VI. Proposed book architecture

1. **Prolegomena: What an atlas of testimony can establish.** Corpus, units of evidence, modes of knowing, and biblical terms.
2. **Thresholds and Conveyance.** Death, displacement, descent, routes, guides, gates, funnels and roads.
3. **The Regions of Perdition.** Pits, cells, walls, valleys, liquids, rivers, fires, darkness, abysses, spatial models.
4. **The Climate of Destruction.** Light, heat, dryness, smell, noise, texture, felt time and perception.
5. **The Inhabitants and Implements.** Human conditions; demonic morphologies; restraints, weapons, inscriptions and guards.
6. **The Operations of Torment.** Mechanisms, repeated episodes, bodily states, deception, memory, conscience, moral reciprocity.

## VII. Mechanical concordance and its limits

The on-demand `atlas_lexical_concordance.csv` is a full-file lexical index produced by `atlas_sift.py` against **every one of the 4,474 source lines**, with 24 source-section boundaries and 35 searchable features. It yields 366 nonempty *source-by-feature* entries. It is a locator, not an attestation table. A line may contain a quoted verse, editorial statement, warning, or a feature observed outside Hell. Inclusion in that spreadsheet does **not** certify that a source witnessed the phenomenon. Close reading and source-specific adjudication control the atlas entries above.

**Current conclusion:** The collection is already sufficient to build a serious first volume's conceptual and bibliographic structure. The strongest descriptive families are thresholds/descent, prisons and pits, oppressive sensorium, creature morphologies, custody, bodily vulnerability, repeated punishment, and continuing memory. The most distinctive witness-specific systems—BAX's anatomical Hell, MEL's simulated cells, PUR's grid-and-river system, and APY's culturally named guide—must retain their own provenance and architecture.
