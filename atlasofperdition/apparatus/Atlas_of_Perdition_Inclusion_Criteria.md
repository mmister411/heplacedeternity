# Atlas of Perdition — Inclusion Measure (AIM 1.0)

**Working definition:** A content-based measure of whether a reported account contributes describable material to *Atlas of Perdition*. It is separate from identifying the speaker, finding a transcript, keeping an archival copy, securing republication rights, or judging whether an extraordinary claim actually occurred.

## First principle

**An available testimony is not necessarily an Atlas testimony. An Atlas-qualifying passage does not require a complete transcript to be identified provisionally.**

**Authorial authority:** Only the Atlas author/user may approve inclusion of a testimony unit, passage, or newly proposed phenomenon. An AIM score is an analytical recommendation and can never itself constitute approval.

Qualification is assessed at the level of a **narrative passage or scene**, not the person's name or a whole book. A mixed heaven-and-hell account may qualify because of one infernal passage; its other scenes retain their own scope. A single person may have several testimony units or versions. A repeated account is not a separate independent witness.

## AIM score (0–4, or U)

| Value | Inclusion meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **U — Unassessed** | No adequate passage, scene description, or attributable excerpt has yet been examined. | Hold as prospect; do not treat an unverified title or search result as either inclusion or exclusion. |
| **0 — Outside scope** | Material examined concerns Heaven, earthly ministry, prophecy, biography, or another subject without an infernal phenomenon or explicit infernal interface. | Retain source lead in the wider collection, not the *Atlas of Perdition* phenomena register. |
| **1 — Infernal reference** | Hell or judgment is named or taught, but no specific reported encounter, feature, process, or condition is described. | Bibliographic/thematic context only. |
| **2 — Encounter claim without extractable detail** | A witness claims an infernal visit, vision, or disclosure but the available passage provides no bounded phenomenon beyond the claim of access. | Candidate for further sourcing; no phenomenon extraction yet. |
| **3 — Descriptive phenomenon** | At least one attributable feature is described: a place, geometry, creature, action, implement, bodily condition, sensory quality, transition, or stated operation. | Eligible for an Atlas entry once the relevant passage can be anchored. |
| **4 — Structured infernal account** | Two or more describable features are related by sequence, position, agency, containment, function, contrast, or another explicit relationship. | Strong eligibility for scene inventory and linked phenomena; extract separately without inventing missing relationships. |

The scale measures **descriptive yield**, not doctrinal agreement, spectacular detail, emotional impact, originality, independent verification, or the length of the account. Repetition across witnesses can matter even if no new category results. A reported feature need not have a biblical analogue to be catalogued; its scriptural relationship is examined later and labeled separately.

## Required locus classification

Record the locus of each qualifying passage as one of:

- **Infernal proper:** the reported scene or condition is explicitly located within Perdition.
- **Threshold / transit:** a described entrance, passage, gate, descent, escape, or boundary explicitly connects to Perdition.
- **Operationally adjacent:** a demon, dispatch, activity, or related phenomenon occurs outside the realm but is expressly tied to its workings. Usually contextual; placement in the Atlas must be justified.
- **Non-infernal:** no such location or relationship.
- **Undetermined:** the witness or passage does not establish the locus.

A scene of heavenly judgment, an earthly warning, or spiritual warfare is not automatically an infernal location. Record it in a related corpus when appropriate.

## Attribution and textual support are separate checks

For each scored passage record:

1. **Who reports it?** Identify the speaker or mediating editor; distinguish the eyewitness from a person repeating another's story.
2. **What mode of knowledge is claimed?** Preserve existing `O`, `F`, `H`, `K`, `S`, and `I` evidence modes.
3. **Where is the passage?** Cite a stable page, chapter, timestamp, paragraph, or source line. A full transcript is not required if the passage is directly accessible and attributable.
4. **What has actually been read or heard?** Record `basis=direct passage`, `basis=audio/video segment`, `basis=attributed extract`, or `basis=secondary synopsis`; do not promote a synopsis to direct witnessing.
5. **What remains unresolved?** Identity, translation, editing, completeness, or transmission questions stay explicit. Unknown provenance does not itself make a descriptive passage score zero.

## Three independent decisions, plus publication rights

| Axis | Question | Sample states |
|---|---|---|
| **AIM / content qualification** | Does the passage yield an attributable infernal phenomenon? | U, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| **Source acquisition** | What source material do we hold or have access to? | Name only; link; excerpt; video/audio segment; complete transcript; preserved authorized copy |
| **Author approval** | Has the Atlas author/user explicitly accepted this specified inclusion? | Not requested; proposed; approved; approved with conditions; deferred; rejected |

**Inclusion workflow:** screen for relevant infernal content; secure an attributable passage; prepare a clearly scoped recommendation; **request explicit approval from the Atlas author/user**. A score of 3–4 establishes **recommendation eligibility only**. Actual admission into the phenomena register additionally requires (a) source anchoring, (b) stable record identification, and **(c) recorded author approval for the material being admitted**. A score of 2 invites targeted acquisition, not admission. An unavailable source remains U, not 0.

**Publication rights:** whether a complete text may be reproduced in a public repository is an additional independent question. Author approval for Atlas inclusion does not imply permission to redistribute the underlying text.

## Required author-approval step

Before adding any new testimony passage or phenomenon to the Atlas apparatus, present an **inclusion packet** containing:

1. Exact testimony identity and version or source artifact, with stable passage/page/timestamp anchors.
2. The passage's AIM score, infernal locus, and explanation of its reported descriptive yield.
3. Proposed phenomena, evidence modes, and any existing or proposed taxonomy/motif relationships.
4. Known uncertainties in attribution, mediation, conflicting versions, and scope.
5. The exact proposed inclusion boundary: which passage(s), scene(s), or record(s) would enter.

The author/user may **approve**, **approve with conditions**, **defer**, or **reject**. Record the decision, decision maker (Atlas author/user), date, reference to the user's explicit instruction, and approved scope. Silence, a high AIM score, the availability of a transcript, or an earlier approval of some other passage cannot be treated as approval.

A decision concerning one account does not automatically cover later extracts, additional testimony versions, or broader claims. Those require separate approval or an explicit scope extension. Work awaiting approval remains in the prospect or proposal register and **must not be inserted into the curated phenomena register, scene inventory, cross-witness concordance, or published Atlas manuscript as admitted material**.

Existing 426 records predate this gate. Preserve them without asserting that explicit approval under this new protocol was recorded; retrospective review is a separate author-directed action.

## Examples of scoring, not judgments about witnesses

- A biographical listing with only a name: **U**.
- A sermon asserting that Hell exists: **1** if it contains no reported infernal feature.
- A personal claim of visiting Hell without further description: **2**.
- A reported experience of screams or extreme heat in an infernal setting: **3**, when the sensory report is attributable.
- A described road leading to stacked cells, with specific beings moving occupants between them: **4**, because it supplies multiple features and their relationships.
- A book of heavenly visions with no infernal portion: **0 for this atlas**, without judgment on its usefulness elsewhere.
