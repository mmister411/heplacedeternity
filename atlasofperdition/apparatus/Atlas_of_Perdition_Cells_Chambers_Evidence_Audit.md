## Scope and evidentiary discipline

This audit examines the **reported structures and operations** of cells, chambers, and custodial rooms. It does not establish the existence of an infernal location, the independence of narratives, or an identity between settings reported by different witnesses. Source-line matches have already been mechanically validated for all 426 register entries; the assessment below is a further check of **what the selected passages actually describe**. Reported observation (`O`), sensation (`F`), privileged knowledge (`K`), and statements attributed to an encountered speaker (`S`) remain distinct.

## Structural findings

1. **The shared element is bounded custody.** All seven units report some sort of cell or enclosed custodial space. That agreement supports a motif family, not a single architectural plan.
2. **Five architectural modes are distinguishable within the family:** (a) a cell with unspecified construction (Brenes and parts of Zambrano), (b) excavated and barred tiers (Baxter), (c) circular stacked cubes with simulated interiors (Melvin), (d) animate or heart-wall compartments (Baxter), and (e) a rough-stone prison room (Wiese). These are descriptive modes, not mutually exclusive doctrinal regions.
3. **Movement and access differ:** approach under escort (Brenes), departure through an apparently ruptured simulated horizon (Melvin), metal door opened by a key-bearing guard (Baxter), escorted entry and return (Zambrano), and sudden placement followed by forced return (Wiese).
4. **Custody has more than one occupant class.** Human prisoners predominate in these passages; Zambrano IV reports chained demons. It would be inaccurate to define all cells as exclusively human holding places.
5. **Reported dimensions must retain their speaker and locus.** Melvin reports six-high stacking. Baxter reports visible circular tiers, while the seventeen-mile height comes from attributed speech. Neither measures or locates the other witness's structures.
6. **The rocking-chair motif requires editorial caution.** Brenes and Baxter both include a woman in a cell whose appearance changes. Baxter also describes a second rocking-chair scene. Similar scene elements should be indexed for textual-source investigation, not counted as proof of independent corroboration or silently treated as one encounter.

## Specific correction to the cross-witness concordance

The previous *Cells and custodial rooms* row included `PER-008` (Jennifer Perez). Its source at **line 2091** describes a person chained at the neck, repeatedly speared and thrown into a **pit of fire**, with no cell or room. That record is properly retained in the existing **Pits, shafts and recesses**, **Chains and physical binding**, and **Tearing, spearing and mutilation** motif families. It has been removed only from *Cells and custodial rooms*. The family therefore now comprises **seven testimony units**, not eight; the source register remains **426 entries**, and the concordance remains **43 motif families**.

This is a **mapping correction**, not a deletion of evidence or a revision to the primary taxonomy.

## Biblical cross-reference boundary

The register links some cell descriptions to **Proverbs 7:27**, **Jonah 2:6**, and **Isaiah 24:22**. These provide language about chambers of death, bars, prisoners, or a pit; none independently specifies the documented variants of numbered cells, six-high cubic tiers, changing scenery, a seventeen-mile block, or soft living walls. The existing classifications of `A` (analogy) or `E` (testimony-specific extension) should be retained pending passage-by-passage exegesis. References to these passages are *correspondences*, not architectural verification.
