# Atlas of Perdition — Scene Inventory

**Working edition:** 16 November 2026  
**Corpus:** The collected testimony file supplied with the project, with the two earlier full-length accounts used as context.  
**Purpose:** A navigable scene index for the close reading of all 16 testimony units, including Mary K. Baxter's infernal chapters and Angelica Zambrano's four reported journeys. The separate *Phenomena Register* contains individually numbered, source-line-anchored observations and statements.

## Reading key

- **Scene:** the location, incident, or successive incidents recounted in a passage.
- **Direct report:** what the narrator describes seeing, hearing, feeling, or undergoing.
- **Attributed explanation:** a statement placed in the mouth of Jesus, another guide, a demon, or an afflicted person. Its content stays attributed to that speaker.
- **Open vision:** material shown within a vision that concerns earth, the future, or a symbolic scene. This is not silently turned into a surveyed room or literal object in Hell.
- **Textual locus:** line positions in the attached collected testimony file; these identify the local collection, not an external edition or publication.

## IV. Shared patterns visible after close extraction

1. **Route becomes custody.** Tunnels, falls, gates, roads and ledges lead to cells, pits, racks or constrained areas. The handover from transit to imprisonment is a recurrent narrative transition.
2. **Architecture is sometimes animate.** Baxter describes a breathing heart and living cells; other testimonies describe fixed constructed compartments. These forms belong together as a comparison, not as one uniform building plan.
3. **The prison has administrators.** Demons appear as escorts, guards, tormentors, strategists, assigned agents and, in Zambrano's fourth account, punished subordinates.
4. **Punishment has memory and repetition.** Prisoners remember lives, speak their old roles, repeat contested acts, undergo renewed bodily harm, or experience recurring staged scenes.
5. **The senses describe a habitat.** Heat, odor, air, sound, thirst, darkness, flame, mud, vermin and touch form a separate environmental taxonomy rather than a single generic idea of torment.
6. **Some visions display rather than locate.** The life-screen, visionary horns, symbolic processions and spirits of people still alive on earth are recorded in their reported mode. They do not automatically establish the literal physical arrangement of the prison.

## V. Open editorial distinctions

- **Rocking-chair parallel:** Carmelo Brenes and Baxter each report a woman in a chair whose apparent form changes, and Baxter includes a further chair episode. These are individually indexed. Their narrative resemblance is clear; the available collection does not settle the relation between their tellings.
- **Degrees and regions:** “heart,” “belly,” “center,” “legs,” “arms,” “jaws,” “outer darkness” and “cells” belong to the accounts' own spatial vocabulary. One should not force their different scales into a mathematically consistent blueprint.
- **Named historical people:** A witness identifying a well-known person among prisoners is a testimony claim. Preserve who reports the identification, the attributed words and the described scene without promoting the claim into a verified historical fact.
- **Scripture:** A verse may directly name a feature (fire, worm, book, pit), support an ethical principle, supply an analogy, or be used to justify an image not described there. Those relations are not equivalent.
- **Unpublished material:** A testimony need not have a known original edition to be included. The local textual anchor and witness attribution supply the working provenance for this collection.

The *Phenomena Register* is the primary catalogue. The *Cross-Witness Concordance* tracks recurring families across reports. This *Scene Inventory* preserves sequence and spatial context that a taxonomy alone cannot hold.
