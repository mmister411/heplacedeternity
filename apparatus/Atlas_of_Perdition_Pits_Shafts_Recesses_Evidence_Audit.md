## Method

The object is the **reported container or opening**, together with its relative position, material, scale, occupants, contents, and operations. Similar vocabulary does not prove one geographic location. A measured-looking quantity may be an estimate, an impression, privileged knowledge, or speech attributed to a guide. The same scene can yield several register entries; those entries are not independent observations. Lines cited below locate the user's compiled text, not a verified external edition.

## Comparative structure

1. **Open shaft or abyss:** Melvin and Zambrano describe downward depth that the narrator cannot measure; Baxter describes a central shaft next to tiers of cells. These are not equivalent to small individually occupied basins.
2. **Discrete ground recesses:** Baxter reports shallow, bowl-shaped pits with an approximate four-by-three-foot profile. Purser reports a larger pool-bearing enclosure in a grid, with a roughly thirty-foot descent and three-foot spacing between neighboring pits. The two layouts should remain distinct.
3. **Liquid or thermal interior:** Brenes uses boiling oil-like substance; Baxter describes brimstone-lined walls and flames; Perez describes a fiery pit; Purser reports a viscous, fiery pool. The sources do not establish a uniform liquid, temperature, or chemical composition.
4. **Material and boundary:** Baxter describes glowing mineral walls in small recesses; Purser describes soft, insect-like walls and interpit ground. These are materially different reports.
5. **Custodial or punitive operation:** Brenes, Perez, and Wiese depict efforts to leave a fire-containing region being prevented, but by different mechanisms: a demon returning escapees, forcible repeated immersion, and guards at a rim. These are operation-level correspondences, not shared architecture.
6. **Scale and count:** The exact numbers belong to their sources and modes. Baxter's dimensions are part of the witness's scene description; Wiese's miles are explicitly claimed knowledge; Purser's pool size and number of pits are comparisons or estimates. None supplies measurements of the others' sites.
7. **Adjacent thresholds:** Baxter's chained door is a boundary feature whose destination is **identified by attributed speech**. It is retained as a qualified link to the pit motif, but it does not constitute a visually surveyed pit.

## Evidence corrections and inclusions

Two entries were wrongly represented as pit structures:

- **BAX-088**, line 1174, describes a large disk with fire at its center and darkness at its edge, held by a figure in an outer-darkness scene. It is not a pit, shaft, or recess. It remains indexed in its other applicable setting.
- **KNI-001**, line 4228, describes the narrator descending **like** a deep pit. This is a felt descent expressed by simile, not a surveyed pit structure. It remains in the descent motif.

Directly relevant records that were missing from this family have been added: **BRE-012** (earth openings), **AZ1-015** and **AZ1-009** (abyss scenes), **BAX-030** (cell-adjacent shaft), **PUR-012** (pit wall material), and **WIE-032** (pit-edge guards).

After these changes, this family contains **16 assigned records across seven testimony units**. The corpus still contains **426 phenomenon records**, **43 motif families**, and **71 second-order subclasses**. This audit changes motif membership, not the taxonomic architecture or primary observations.
